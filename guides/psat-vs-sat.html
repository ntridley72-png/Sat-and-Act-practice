PSAT vs SAT: What Actually Differs

The PSAT is close enough to the SAT that preparing for one prepares you for the other. The differences are small but they matter in two specific places: how to read your score, and whether you are in range for National Merit money.

Everything about the testing experience — the Bluebook app, the adaptive modules, the built-in Desmos calculator, the question styles — is shared. If you have sat one, you know what the other feels like.

Is the PSAT easier?

Slightly, and only at the top. The PSAT omits the most difficult content, which is why its ceiling is 1520 rather than 1600. For most students in the middle of the distribution the difficulty is effectively identical. What this means practically: a student scoring 1100 on the PSAT is seeing essentially the same test as the SAT, while a student scoring 1500 on the PSAT has not yet been tested on the hardest material they will face.

The third column assumes eight to twelve weeks of structured practice. It is an expectation, not a promise.

The useful way to use your PSAT: treat it as a free, officially-administered baseline. It is the most accurate diagnostic you will get without sitting the real SAT. Compare the result against the reported ranges at the colleges on your list and you have a target before eleventh grade is over.

National Merit: the part that is worth real money

The eleventh-grade PSAT/NMSQT is the qualifying test for the National Merit Scholarship Program. Tenth-grade sittings do not count.

Qualification does not use your 1520-scale total. It uses the Selection Index: add your three section subscores (Reading, Writing and Language, Math, each 8–38) and multiply by two, for a maximum of 228.

StageRoughly who
CommendedTop ~50,000 nationally; a single national Selection Index cutoff
SemifinalistTop ~16,000; cutoff set per state, typically about 207–223
FinalistMost semifinalists who complete the application
ScholarAbout 7,500 who receive an award

State cutoffs vary substantially and move every year — a score that qualifies in one state may fall short in another. Beyond the National Merit award itself, a number of universities offer large automatic scholarships to Finalists, sometimes full tuition. If you are within a few index points of your state's recent cutoff, the eleventh-grade PSAT is one of the highest-value tests you will ever sit. Browse other scholarships and their requirements.

Should you prepare for the PSAT?

It depends entirely on one question: are you plausibly in National Merit range?

  • If your tenth-grade PSAT or a practice test puts you near your state's cutoff — within about 10 index points — then yes, prepare seriously. The financial return is larger than almost anything else available to a high school junior.
  • If you are not close, do not prepare for the PSAT specifically. Prepare for the SAT. The work transfers almost completely, and the SAT is the score that actually reaches colleges.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between the PSAT and the SAT?

Same format and question style; the PSAT tops out at 1520 rather than 1600, is slightly easier at the top, and is not sent to colleges. Its main consequence is National Merit qualification in eleventh grade.

Do colleges see your PSAT score?

No. The only way a PSAT reaches an admissions office is indirectly, through National Merit recognition.

How do you convert a PSAT score to an SAT score?

Roughly one-to-one — a 1300 PSAT is about a 1300 SAT with no further prep — and most students gain points between the two sittings.

What PSAT score do you need for National Merit?

Semifinalist cutoffs use the Selection Index (out of 228) and are set per state, typically falling between about 207 and 223 and changing each year.

Does the tenth-grade PSAT count?

Not for National Merit. Only the eleventh-grade PSAT/NMSQT qualifies; earlier sittings are diagnostic.
