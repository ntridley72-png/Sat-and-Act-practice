Scholarships for High School Seniors

Senior year is the highest-yield scholarship window — and the easiest to fumble by applying in random order. Prioritize like this:

1. Automatic and state awards first

Check what your state and your likely colleges award for GPA and test scores. These often have the best ratio of money to effort.

2. Local and employer awards second

Community foundations, parents' employers, unions, and civic clubs see far fewer applicants than national programs.

3. National programs third

Pick two or three where you genuinely fit the mission, and do them properly.

The essay strategy

Write one 600-word master essay about a specific experience and what it changed. Then write two shorter variants: one about service, one about goals. Almost every prompt can be answered by re-cutting these three.

Deadline discipline

Most senior awards cluster October through February. Put every deadline in a calendar with reminders 14 days ahead, and submit a week early.
