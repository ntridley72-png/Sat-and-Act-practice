Test-Optional Colleges Explained: Should You Submit Your SAT or ACT?

Many colleges let you apply without SAT or ACT scores, but "test-optional" does not mean scores do not matter. This guide explains the policy types and a simple rule for deciding whether to submit.

The policy types

  • Required: you must submit an SAT or ACT score.
  • Recommended or considered: scores are looked at when submitted.
  • Test-optional: you choose whether to send scores. A strong score can still help, and some scholarships and programs still ask for one.
  • Test-blind: scores are not considered at all, even if you send them.

The U.S. Department of Education reports each college's testing policy in IPEDS, and our college profiles show it when it is reported.

Should you submit your score?

  1. Find the college's middle 50% range on its profile.
  2. If your score is at or above the midpoint, submitting usually helps.
  3. If it is well below the 25th percentile, consider applying without it, if the college is truly test-optional.
  4. Check scholarships: merit awards at some colleges still use test scores.
  5. Check programs such as honors colleges, nursing or engineering that may require scores even when the college does not.

Frequently asked questions

What does test-optional mean?

It means you decide whether to submit SAT or ACT scores with your application. If you submit them, the college will consider them; if you do not, you are still evaluated.

Should I submit my SAT score to a test-optional college?

Usually yes if it is at or above the college's median score, since it can strengthen your application. Consider leaving it out if it is well below the 25th percentile.

Do test-optional colleges still give merit aid based on scores?

Some do. Check each college's scholarship page, because scores can still unlock aid even when they are optional for admission.

What is the difference between test-optional and test-blind?

A test-optional college lets you choose whether to send scores and considers them if you do. A test-blind college does not consider scores at all.

Where can I find a college's testing policy?

On the college's admissions website, and on our college profiles where the policy is reported by the college to the U.S. Department of Education.

Do I need a test score for scholarships?

Often, for merit awards. Review each scholarship's requirements, and keep a practice score that you can improve.
