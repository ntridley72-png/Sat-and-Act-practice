One profile, one list

“Your profile” and “How your scores compare” are currently two screens that ask for the same facts and answer the same question twice. Four ways to make them one.

Two encodings of the same estimate is the subtle one: a 63% badge and a “Likely” pill are the same number wearing different clothes, and a reader has to learn both.

A · Profile on top, list below

one column

The profile collapses to the four facts that actually move the estimate — score, GPA, grade, rigor — as a header strip. Everything else (major, plan, essays, class rank, state) lives behind Edit full profile, open in place. The sidebar of saved colleges is gone: the list below is the saved colleges.

Trade-off: the form pushes the list down — on a laptop you land on inputs, not answers.

B · Sticky “you” rail beside the list

recommended

The rail becomes the single home for everything about you and nothing else — ring, the inputs that drive the estimate, and More about you holding the rest. Filters move out of the rail into a bar over the list, so the rail never mixes “who I am” with “what I'm looking at”. Change GPA and watch the verdicts move beside it.

Trade-off: the rail gets tall, and under 1024 it has to stack above the list.

C · Shared header, two tabs

least rebuild

One header owns every fact that was duplicated — ring, score, chips, Use predicted — and it stays put across both tabs. Your list and Your profile then hold only what is unique to each, so nothing appears twice without either screen being restructured.

Trade-off: a tab is a mode — you can't watch a field change the list, which is the main reason to merge at all.

D · List is the screen, profile is a drawer

most editing work

The list is the whole product. The facts that drive it sit in the top bar as editable chips — click GPA 3.3 and type over it. The long tail of the form lives in a drawer you open only when you need it, so a 12-field form never competes with the answers.

Trade-off: inline chip editing is the biggest build of the four, and the full form is cramped in a 360px drawer.

The saved-colleges sidebar disappears in every option — it was a worse copy of the list it sat next to. The fit percentage and the verdict pill travel together on one row, so the estimate is stated once. Filters never sit in the same container as your own details.

B is the recommendation: it is the only one where a field and the thing it changes are visible at the same time, which is the whole argument for merging the screens. C is the cheapest path if the rebuild has to be small.
