R1 · One number, then what to do about it

recommended

The estimate is the screen, so it gets the size — with the change since last time attached to it instead of sitting in its own notice three blocks down. The domain table becomes what to work on, with the drill already set up on each row: the question a student has after a score is “now what”, and the answer was previously a read-only table. Session rewards drop to one quiet line; they were a titled box competing with the score.

Trade-off: the full domain breakdown moves behind a disclosure, so a student who wants every number has one more tap.

S1 · Resume first, setup folded away

recommended

There is one orange button on the screen. Today there are two for the same action — “Start a practice test” inside the score card and “Start practice→” under the setup — plus orange step badges, orange tile labels and orange footer links, about twenty in all. For a returning student the setup is already decided, so it collapses to a line you can read and change. The three feature cards become a quiet row of links rather than three cards competing with the primary.

Trade-off: a first-time visitor has to open the setup to see what the choices are, so the collapsed state has to state them in full.

S2 · Setup as one row, no step numbers

setup visible

The three choices are independent and all visible at once, so numbering them 1 / 2 / 3 is decoration — it tells the reader a sequence exists where none does. They become one row of three segmented controls with the summary and the single primary beneath. The labels also stop wrapping: “Reading & Writing” currently breaks to three lines inside its segment at 320px while the other two sit on one.

Trade-off: the row stacks to three full-width controls under 860px, which is taller than the collapsed line in S1.

U1 · Lead with what is weak

recommended

“Suggested for you” already exists but sits below the full wall of skill chips, which is the wrong way round: a student opening this screen mostly does not know what to pick, and the app already knows. The three suggestions come first as startable cards carrying the accuracy that earned them the spot. The complete browser stays, one domain per row, collapsed with counts.

Trade-off: someone who knows exactly which skill they want has to open a domain row to reach the chip.

U2 · Skill table with your accuracy

for a returning user

Every skill in one sortable table against what you have actually scored on it, with the drill on the row. Denser than U1 and better the tenth time you open the screen: it answers “where am I weakest across everything” rather than “what should I do next”.

Trade-off: a first-time user sees a table of dashes, because no skill has any accuracy yet.
